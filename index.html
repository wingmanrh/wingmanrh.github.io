How it works

Three steps. Then it's out of everyone's hands.

1

Match

You post your token launch. Someone with proven reach agrees to promote it, and you settle on how to split the creator fees.

2

Lock

One transaction locks that split into a contract with both wallets baked in. From then on, nobody can change it, not even us.

3

Launch

Your token trades on pools.trade. Creator fees flow into the contract, and each side withdraws its share on its own, forever.

Straight answers

Questions everyone asks.

Where does the money come from?
pools.trade pays token creators 40% of the ETH side of the 0.25% swap fee, on buy volume. The more your token trades, the more both sides earn, straight from the market.
What does Wingman charge?
Founding deals are free, forever. Later deals include a 5% platform share, taken on-chain in the same contract, visible to everyone.
What if a partner takes the deal and never posts?
Nothing locks until your partner has agreed to the deal and you both know exactly what gets posted and when — the lock and the launch happen together. After that, a partner only earns if your token actually trades, so ghosting pays them nothing. And anyone who ghosts a deal is out of Wingman for good.
My token is already live. Can I still use Wingman?
Yes. Choose “Already live” when you submit. We look at your token's on-chain record instead of your wallet's history, and everything else works the same: a partner agrees, the split locks, they push.
What does a reach partner actually do?
Whatever you two agree on before anything locks — usually a post at launch and real attention through the first days. The terms are set person to person; the contract only handles the money.
Why only Robinhood Chain?
It's where the launch volume is right now, around 4,600 launches a day. We'll add more chains when it makes sense; the contracts work anywhere Uniswap's launchpad runs.
Who are you?
Anonymous, and it doesn't matter. Nothing here relies on trusting us: every number we publish is measured on-chain, and every promise is enforced by code you can read.
